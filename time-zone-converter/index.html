Why fixed offsets fail

Most of the year, New York is 5 hours behind London. But the US springs forward on the second Sunday of March while the UK waits until the last Sunday — so for about three weeks the gap narrows to 4 hours, and it narrows to 4 again for the week in autumn when the UK falls back before the US does. Phoenix never moves at all, so its difference with Denver changes twice a year even though neither city moved relative to the sun. A converter that stores one offset per city gets all of these wrong; this one stores no offsets at all — it asks the IANA time zone database, the same source your phone uses.

Frequently asked questions

How does the time zone converter handle daylight saving time?

It converts using the IANA time zone database rules for the exact date you enter. If one city has sprung forward and the other has not — like New York and London for a few weeks each March — the difference changes automatically (4 hours instead of the usual 5).

Why does the time difference between two cities change during the year?

Because cities change clocks on different dates, and some — Phoenix, Honolulu, Tokyo, Dubai — never change them at all. Any fixed offset table goes stale; this converter always uses the rules in force on your date.

What does “next day” mean in a conversion?

Converting across the International Date Line can land on a different calendar day. 8:00 PM in New York can already be 10:00 AM the next day in Sydney — the converter states the day shift explicitly.
