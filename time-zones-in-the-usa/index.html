Time Zones in the USA, Explained

The 50 states span six time zones; counting US territories, federal law defines nine.

EasternEST — UTC−5EDT — UTC−4New York

Territories add Atlantic Time (Puerto Rico, US Virgin Islands — UTC−4, no DST), Samoa Time (UTC−11) and Chamorro Time (Guam — UTC+10, on the other side of the date line).

Split states

Thirteen states sit in two zones — Florida's panhandle, Texas (El Paso is Mountain), Kentucky, Tennessee, Nebraska, Kansas, the Dakotas and others. County-level lines are set by the US Department of Transportation, which is why the zone map has such wiggly borders. Arizona is the famous special case: entirely Mountain, but no daylight saving.

Frequently asked questions

How many time zones are in the USA?

Six across the 50 states (Eastern, Central, Mountain, Pacific, Alaska, Hawaii-Aleutian) and nine by law including the territories.

Which US time zone is UTC−5?

Eastern Standard Time in winter; Central Daylight Time also lands on UTC−5 in summer.

Which states are in two time zones?

Thirteen states are split, including Florida, Texas, Kentucky, Tennessee, Indiana, Michigan and the Dakotas.
