About WhatTime

WhatTime answers one of the most-asked questions on the internet — "what time is it in…?" — and answers it correctly, including the part most world-clock sites get wrong: daylight saving time.

How it works

Every clock and conversion on this site is computed live, in your browser, from the IANA time zone database — the official rulebook of the world's time zones, maintained from government decisions and used by every major operating system, phone network and airline. Your browser implements it through the standard Intl API. WhatTime stores no fixed offsets: when a government moves a clock-change date, the database update flows to your browser and this site is right automatically. The static facts on city pages (standard/daylight offsets, 2026 clock-change dates) are generated from the same IANA database and re-verified at build time.

Why it matters

Time differences are not fixed. New York is 5 hours behind London most of the year, 4 hours for a few weeks each spring, and 6 for a week each autumn. Phoenix and Denver share a time in winter and differ in summer, because Arizona never changes clocks. Sites that hard-code one offset per city are wrong for weeks every year; WhatTime was built specifically to be right on those weeks too.

Corrections and updates

Time zone rules change when governments legislate — Russia, Mexico, Brazil and Türkiye have all abolished or altered clock changes in the last decade. If you spot a fact on this site that disagrees with an official source, we want to know; pages are regenerated whenever the underlying zone data or our facts change.
