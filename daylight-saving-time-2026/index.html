Daylight Saving Time 2026: US Start and End Dates

Daylight saving time (DST) in the United States begins on Sunday, March 8, 2026 — clocks spring forward one hour at 2:00 AM local time — and ends on Sunday, November 1, 2026, when clocks fall back one hour at 2:00 AM.

US daylight saving dates

YearDST starts (spring forward)DST ends (fall back)
2026Sunday, March 8Sunday, November 1
2027Sunday, March 14Sunday, November 7

By federal law (the Energy Policy Act of 2005), US daylight saving runs from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November. At 2:00 AM clocks jump straight to 3:00 AM in spring — that hour simply does not exist — and in autumn 2:00 AM becomes 1:00 AM, so the 1:00 hour happens twice.

Who does not observe daylight saving

Arizona (except the Navajo Nation) and Hawaii stay on standard time all year, as do Puerto Rico, Guam, the US Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands. Indiana observed DST statewide only since 2006 — a reminder that the map has changed within living memory. See our dedicated pages on Arizona and Hawaii.

Why the dates matter for conversion

Europe changes clocks on the last Sundays of March and October, so for roughly three weeks each spring and one week each autumn the US–Europe time difference is an hour off its usual value. Our converter applies the real rules for any date, so those awkward weeks are handled automatically.

Frequently asked questions

When does daylight saving time start in 2026?

In the United States, DST starts on Sunday, March 8, 2026, at 2:00 AM local time, when clocks spring forward to 3:00 AM.

When does daylight saving time end in 2026?

DST ends on Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 AM local time, when clocks fall back to 1:00 AM.

Do all US states observe daylight saving time?

No. Arizona (except the Navajo Nation) and Hawaii do not, and neither do the US territories. Every other state does.
