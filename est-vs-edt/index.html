EST vs EDT: The Difference, Explained

EST is Eastern Standard Time — UTC−5, in effect in winter. EDT is Eastern Daylight Time — UTC−4, in effect during daylight saving. They are the same time zone (Eastern) at two times of year, not two different zones.

AbbreviationFull nameUTC offsetIn effect (US, 2026)
ESTEastern Standard TimeUTC−5Nov 2, 2025 – Mar 7, 2026, then from Nov 1, 2026
EDTEastern Daylight TimeUTC−4Mar 8 – Oct 31, 2026

The common mistake

People write "EST" all year round, but from March to November the East Coast is on EDT — an hour ahead of true EST. Meeting invites that say "3 PM EST" in July are ambiguous by a full hour. The safe habit: write "Eastern" or "ET" when you mean the zone, and reserve EST/EDT for the specific offset. This site never makes you guess: every US clock here shows the abbreviation actually in force today, and the converter applies the right one for your date.

The same pattern repeats across the US: CST/CDT, MST/MDT, PST/PDT, AKST/AKDT. See Time Zones in the USA.

Frequently asked questions

Is New York on EST or EDT right now?

EDT (UTC−4) from the second Sunday of March to the first Sunday of November, and EST (UTC−5) the rest of the year. The live New York clock on this site shows which one applies today.

Which is ahead, EST or EDT?

EDT is one hour ahead of EST — clocks spring forward from EST to EDT in March.

Does Florida use EST and EDT?

Most of Florida follows the Eastern pattern (EST/EDT); the panhandle west of the Apalachicola River is on Central time (CST/CDT).
