Does Arizona Observe Daylight Saving Time?

No. Arizona stays on Mountain Standard Time (MST, UTC−7) all year and has not sprung forward or fallen back since 1968. The one exception is the Navajo Nation in the state's northeast corner, which does observe daylight saving time (Mountain Daylight Time, UTC−6) to stay in step with its lands in Utah and New Mexico — which means the surrounding Hopi Reservation, which follows Arizona, can be an hour behind an enclave inside itself.

Why Arizona opted out

Arizona tried DST in 1967 and dropped it after one summer: in a desert state where daytime highs pass 110°F, pushing sunset an hour later meant air conditioners ran longer into the evening and children waited for school buses in hotter mornings. The state exercised the opt-out the federal Uniform Time Act allows and never went back.

What it does to time differences

In winter, Phoenix and Denver show the same time (both UTC−7). In summer Denver springs forward to UTC−6 and is an hour ahead of Phoenix — while Phoenix matches Los Angeles instead (both UTC−7). Our Phoenix clock and the converter handle this automatically for any date.

Frequently asked questions

Does Phoenix change its clocks?

No. Phoenix stays on Mountain Standard Time (UTC−7) all year.

Is Arizona on Pacific Time in summer?

Effectively, yes — when California springs forward to UTC−7 (PDT), it matches Arizona's year-round UTC−7, though Arizona's zone is still officially Mountain Standard Time.

Does any part of Arizona observe DST?

Yes — the Navajo Nation in northeastern Arizona observes daylight saving time.
