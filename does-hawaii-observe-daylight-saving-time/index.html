Does Hawaii Observe Daylight Saving Time?

No. Hawaii keeps Hawaii Standard Time (HST, UTC−10) all year and does not change its clocks. Close to the equator, Hawaii's sunrise and sunset barely move across the year, so shifting clocks would buy almost nothing — the state formally opted out in 1967.

Hawaii vs the mainland

Because the mainland springs forward and Hawaii does not, the gap changes twice a year. Honolulu is 2 hours behind Los Angeles in winter and 3 hours behind in summer (Los Angeles moves from UTC−8 to UTC−7; Honolulu stays at UTC−10). Against New York: New York is UTC−4 in summer and UTC−5 in winter, so Honolulu is 6 hours behind New York in summer and 5 hours behind in winter. The rule that never changes: Hawaii never moves, everyone else does. The Honolulu clock and the converter always show the live answer for any date.

Frequently asked questions

What time zone is Hawaii in?

Hawaii Standard Time (HST), UTC−10, all year.

When did Hawaii stop observing daylight saving?

Hawaii opted out under the Uniform Time Act in 1967 and has not changed clocks since.

How many hours behind New York is Hawaii?

6 hours during mainland daylight time (roughly March–November) and 5 hours the rest of the year, because New York moves and Hawaii does not.
